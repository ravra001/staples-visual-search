The problem

You know it when you see it. You don't know what to type.

"Mid-century curved-back accent chair in ash grey" is not how anyone shops. A customer who sees a chair they like — in a friend's apartment, a magazine, a photo they took — has to translate what they're looking at into a search box's language, and that translation step is where intent leaks away. A camera icon skips it: the photo is the query.

What we built

Six ways to search, one shared engine underneath

Every mode below is a variation on the same core idea — turn something into a vector, compare it to the catalog — not six separate features bolted together. That's why adding text refinement to "Find Similar" after it already worked for photo search was a small change, not a new subsystem.

Visual Search

Upload a photo of any chair, lamp, rug, or décor item and get back the closest real matches from 10,000 products, ranked by CLIP embedding similarity — with an honest "no strong matches" state when the item genuinely isn't in the catalog, instead of forcing a bad guess.

Zero-typing product discovery

Text Refinement

Found a chair you like the shape of, but want it in black? Type it. The refinement blends into the visual query rather than replacing it, and is constrained to only reorder items that already matched — it can't wander into unrelated results the way a naive text search can.

Refine without losing the visual match

Find Similar

Every product page and product card has a one-click "Find similar," plus a "Similar for less" rail — the same visual neighborhood, filtered to strictly cheaper alternatives. No upload needed; it queries from the product's own vector, already computed.

Cross-sell & price-conscious upsell, automatically

Shop the Room

Upload a photo of a whole room and get back one best match per item detected — the sofa, the rug, the lamp, the wall art — with a running total and one "Add all to cart" button. Not a pile of near-duplicates of whatever object was biggest in frame.

One photo → a filled, multi-item cart

Crop-to-Search

A photo with more than one object in it embeds as a blend of everything in frame. Drag a box around just the item you mean, and only that region gets searched — works with touch, not just a mouse.

Handles real, messy, multi-object photos

Cart & Price Intent

"Under $50" and "cheaper" actually filter and sort by real price data — never sent through the ML model, because price isn't something CLIP was ever trained to know. A real cart page tracks line items, quantities, and a live total, not just a badge count.

ML for what it's good at, plain data for the rest

Why it matters commercially

This isn't a vision demo. It's a shopping-funnel story.

Conversion

Shortens intent-to-cart

Every extra step between "I saw something I want" and "it's in my cart" is a chance to lose the customer. A photo replaces a guessed search query with the exact thing they're looking at.

Average order value

Shop the Room sells the room, not the item

A single photo becomes a multi-item basket with one click — rug, lamp, and wall art added together, not hunted down one search at a time.

Trust & return rate

Honest confidence, not forced results

The threshold-gated "no strong matches" state and calibrated match percentages mean a customer only sees a "match" when it's a real one — the same discipline that keeps returns down when customers buy based on visual similarity.

Retention

"Find similar" turns a dead end into a browse path

Instead of leaving when an item's out of stock or over budget, a customer gets a "similar for less" rail on every product — keeping them shopping instead of leaving the site.

Not a demo — a deployment

Running on real GCP infrastructure, with real production bugs found and fixed

This is deployed on Cloud Run, backed by a real Cloud SQL + pgvector instance seeded with all 10,000 products. Building it for real — not just describing the architecture — is what surfaced these:

✓ HNSW recall bug, found & fixed

pgvector's default search breadth missed true nearest-neighbors on this catalog size — caught by comparing against an exact in-memory search, fixed with a query-time tuning parameter.

✓ N+1 seeding bug, found & fixed

A naive per-row check took over 20 minutes against remote Cloud SQL before a single row committed. Batched inserts brought 9,967 rows down to 143 seconds.

✓ Two backends, verified identical

The in-memory dev backend and the live Postgres/pgvector backend are checked to produce byte-for-byte identical rankings for the same query — not assumed, tested.

✓ CI/CD + CDN, shipped and verified

Every push to main now auto-builds and auto-deploys via Cloud Build. Product images are fronted by their own HTTPS load balancer + Cloud CDN with a Google-managed cert — verified live, not just wired up.

✓ Vertex AI benchmarked, not just "coded"

Head-to-head against local CLIP on 300 real products: Vertex wins recall@1 (80.0% vs 72.7%), but CLIP ties or leads at recall@5/8 — the more representative metric for a results-grid app — at 1/3 the latency and zero marginal cost. Local CLIP stays.
